# MP3 decoder: thirteenth optimization pass

The production baseline is `09a8de397`. This pass replaces MPEG-1 short-block
three-factor reads with six-factor reads, constructs per-band half-integer
attenuations through binary exponents, and caches eight rounded gain/attenuation
products. Production changes are confined to `scalefactors.rs` and
`spectral.rs`. All changes use safe Rust and preserve public signatures,
Huffman/coefficient tables, generators and decoder fields.

## Measurement method

The cumulative stages are `round13-final-base`, `round13-packed`,
`round13-attenuation`, and `round13-combined`. They use the same 187 workloads,
thirteen fixtures, fixed Cargo build path, compiler, configuration and bench
profile. The driver validates source, executable, helper and fixture hashes.
Seven fresh processes per stage rotate their order. Each process reports the
median of eleven batches after warmup, using `QueryThreadCycleTime` pinned to
Windows logical CPU 2. Our compilation and correctness checks run outside
decision timings. Other workspace activity was present; this is not a claim of
an otherwise idle machine. Capture-time initial samples are excluded.

The 5,236 process/workload observations are in [raw data](2026-10-29-mp3-round13.tsv)
and [manifests, medians, ranges and MAD](2026-10-29-mp3-round13.json).
Three added nonzero-factor probes exercise alternating integer/half-integer
exponents, subblock gains and a fractional global gain. Requantization processes
576 coefficients per operation. Scalefactors process one granule/channel;
bit-reader probes process 512 fields; Huffman probes decode 256 symbols.
Fixture construction is untimed. Throughput below is the inverse cycle ratio;
nanoseconds remain in the raw data. No process observations are discarded.

## 1. Read six MPEG-1 short-block factors at once

The two width groups borrow flat destination spans as fixed six-element
chunks. Independent bit shifts unpack each full chunk; only the mixed-block
three-value tail uses the existing variable-length helper. Six maximum-width
four-bit factors fit in 24 bits.
Pure-short blocks use six packed reads instead of twelve when both widths are
nonzero. With both widths nonzero, mixed blocks use five short-factor reads
instead of nine, alongside
their unchanged eight-long-factor read. Width-zero skips, band boundaries,
transmitted widths and transactional failure behavior remain unchanged.
This removes half the packed reads in pure-short blocks. Fixed-size unpacking
lets the compiler unroll the shifts without the helper's dependent shift loop.
The explicit full-chunk/tail paths add three net production lines.

| Input | Before cycles | After cycles | Reduction | CPU throughput |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Pure short, compression 4 | 124.98 | 111.09 | 11.1% | 1.125x |
| Pure short, compression 5 | 191.36 | 169.39 | 11.5% | 1.130x |
| Pure short, compression 15 | 197.00 | 161.99 | 17.8% | 1.216x |
| Mixed, compression 4 | 131.63 | 145.29 | -10.4% | 0.906x |
| Mixed, compression 5 | 197.22 | 192.74 | 2.3% | 1.023x |
| Mixed, compression 15 | 194.94 | 196.41 | -0.8% | 0.993x |

Pure-short medians improve 11.1-17.8%. Mixed compression 4 regresses 10.4%,
while mixed compression 15 is effectively flat (0.8% worse). Mixed blocks do
not show the same benefit: their long section stays unchanged and their tail
still uses the variable-length helper. These costs remain in the raw data.
The change is retained for the pure-short gains and reduced packed-read count.

## 2. Construct per-band attenuation through binary exponents

The attenuation is `2^(-exponent/2)`. An even exponent needs the mantissa of
1.0; an odd exponent uses one cached runtime result of `exp2(-0.5)`. Subtracting
`floor(exponent/2)` from the floating-point exponent constructs the result with
integer operations. The original multiplication by global gain stays in place.

Public u8 scale factors and subblock gains bound the attenuation exponent to
0 through 1,530, so every attenuation is normal. The smallest is `2^-765`.
This handles the full public input range, including values beyond encoded
bitstream limits. Runtime math supplies the mantissa; it is not a hardcoded
rounded decimal. The intermediate stage tests all 1,531 exponents against the
original platform `exp2` expression. The final cross-product test also covers
every gain.

| Input | Before cycles | After cycles | Reduction | CPU throughput |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Long, nonzero factors | 1,625.18 | 1,559.99 | 4.0% | 1.042x |
| Pure short, nonzero factors | 2,760.65 | 2,643.44 | 4.2% | 1.044x |
| Mixed, nonzero factors | 2,667.58 | 2,538.30 | 4.8% | 1.051x |
| Long, zero factors | 2,393.21 | 2,428.99 | -1.5% | 0.985x |
| Pure short, zero factors | 3,251.23 | 3,486.10 | -7.2% | 0.933x |
| Mixed, zero factors | 3,156.85 | 3,260.01 | -3.3% | 0.968x |

The nonzero-factor medians improve 4.0-4.8%. Zero-factor dense cases regress
1.5-7.2%; wide-coefficient controls regress 8.6-11.7%. Several sparse cases
also worsen. This comparison includes process and compiler-layout variation:
although its scalefactor source is unchanged from the packed stage, MPEG-1
pure-short compression 15 rises 25.3%, with a different emitted body and a
408-to-456-byte stack reservation. The earlier intermediate comparison showed
14.3-17.0% lower nonzero-factor cycles for the same attenuation algorithm.
No isolated per-call saving or universal speedup is inferred from these data.

## 3. Cache rounded products for all gain/attenuation phases

Global gain is `2^((gain-210)/4)`. Four fractional phases and two attenuation
parities need only eight rounded products. The initializer evaluates the
original platform gain and half-power expressions, then multiplies them in
the same order as the original requantizer. At runtime, gain selects a pair;
attenuation parity selects its mantissa, and integer operations adjust the
binary exponent. This removes the last per-granule `exp2` call and every
per-band floating-point amplitude multiplication.

Caching the products preserves their multiplication rounding. Simply
combining the exponents and evaluating one power would change some bits.
Power-of-two shifts commute with rounding throughout this normal range:
the smallest amplitude is about `2^-817.5`, and the largest `2^11.25`.
The final independent test compares all **391,936** gain/exponent combinations
against the original two `exp2` expressions and multiplication.

The power cache and product table share one lazy initializer, removing the
second guard from the attenuation stage. Numeric payload grows from 128 to
192 bytes versus baseline: 64 additional bytes. The sixteen coefficient
`powf` values retain their runtime evaluation. Initialization also makes five
`exp2` calls and four multiplications to prepare the additional products.

| Input | Before cycles | After cycles | Reduction | CPU throughput |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Long, nonzero factors | 1,559.99 | 1,469.55 | 5.8% | 1.062x |
| Pure short, nonzero factors | 2,643.44 | 2,389.04 | 9.6% | 1.106x |
| Mixed, nonzero factors | 2,538.30 | 2,394.09 | 5.7% | 1.060x |
| Long, only first coefficient | 1,321.61 | 1,080.60 | 18.2% | 1.223x |
| Pure short, only first coefficient | 1,288.68 | 1,292.20 | -0.3% | 0.997x |
| Mixed, only first coefficient | 1,260.15 | 1,305.81 | -3.6% | 0.965x |

Nonzero-factor medians improve another 5.7-9.6%. Zero-factor dense cases
improve 9.2-15.4%. Sparse short controls are counterexamples: a pure-short first
coefficient is 0.3% slower and a mixed first coefficient 3.6% slower than the
attenuation stage. Against the original baseline, those two final medians
regress 5.4% (1,226.13 to 1,292.20 cycles) and 18.1% (1,105.40 to 1,305.81).
Several unchanged controls also move materially, so the recorded reductions
include layout and process variation rather than only arithmetic savings.

## Rejected alternatives

The separate three-process screen used `round13-base`, `round13-factors`,
`round13-reader`, and `round13-amplitude`, each with 184 workloads. Its 2,208
observations are not pooled with final measurements.

Forced inlining of the packed-factor helper removed its call boundary but gave
mixed results: pure-short MPEG-1 compression 15 rose from 188.51 to 234.87
cycles (24.6% worse). Forcing the general reader inline after that rose from
146.12 to 215.33 cycles for MPEG-1 long compression 15 (47.4% worse).
Several unchanged controls also varied materially, and the screen retained
that dispersion. Neither attribute is kept.

A zero-exponent amplitude branch matched output exactly, but was superseded by
the general exponent construction, which also handles nonzero exponents
without a math-library call. The screen already included the expanded
all-gain/zero-factor verification. [Screen raw data](2026-10-29-mp3-round13-screen.tsv),
[summary/manifests](2026-10-29-mp3-round13-screen.json),
[code generation](2026-10-29-mp3-round13-screen-codegen.json),
[corpus check](2026-10-29-mp3-round13-screen-corpus.json).

The subsequent seven-process intermediate comparison used the same 187 final
workloads. A dynamic six-factor unpacking loop was insufficient: pure-short
compression 15 improved only 2.0%, while mixed compression 4 regressed 7.3%.
The final variant uses fixed-size chunks and independent shifts instead.
A separate four-entry global-gain cache eliminated the remaining gain `exp2`
call but regressed nonzero-factor requantization 1.7-2.7%; it is replaced by
the rounded-product table. No intermediate observations are pooled with the
final set. [Intermediate raw data](2026-10-29-mp3-round13-intermediate.tsv),
[summary/manifests](2026-10-29-mp3-round13-intermediate.json),
[cold data](2026-10-29-mp3-round13-intermediate-cold.tsv),
[code generation](2026-10-29-mp3-round13-intermediate-codegen.json),
[corpus check](2026-10-29-mp3-round13-intermediate-corpus.json).

## Exact behavior and checks

All four final stages produce the same expanded 13-fixture trace:
**662,735,590 bytes**, SHA-256
`878c81985e0638c5efe4a8b63e1f2873ca93952f2c7652c897f18edf7cb5f9ee`.
The 80-file comparison produces **687,503,590 bytes**, SHA-256
`6f95a67e0642aa4abbf34eb08750aa66d2ae4f4b490d5f0c62666f8552cf48a1`.
[Commands, fixture hashes and matching outputs](2026-10-29-mp3-round13-corpus.json).

The verifier retains the full previous bit-reader/Huffman/CRC, side-info,
scalefactor, requantization, synthesis, nonfinite-payload and file PCM checks.
It adds 11,520 zero-factor vectors: every public gain, nine sample rates and
five block shapes, including full signed-i16 coefficient limits and NaN-poisoned
destinations. Prior gain-varying vectors also exercise full u8 factors and
subblock gains, both scale flags, preflags and zero input. The cross-product
unit test covers every attenuation exponent with every gain on the current
platform math implementation.

All 71 tests, strict all-target Clippy, and crate formatting pass.
[Validation commands, logs and source hashes](2026-10-29-mp3-round13-checks.json).
Measurements are specific to the recorded Windows x86-64/MSVC compiler and host;
the exhaustive tests also run when the crate is tested on another platform.
